Positive phototropism (Cholodny–Went hypothesis)

Auxin (indole-3-acetic acid, IAA) is synthesised at the shoot apex. Blue light from one side is perceived by phototropin photoreceptors, and auxin is redistributed laterally to the shaded flank. Cells on the shaded flank elongate faster than those on the lit flank, so the shoot bends towards the light.

The bend develops in the elongation zone below the apex, after a short lag. A shoot lit from directly above receives no lateral signal, distributes auxin evenly and grows vertically, which is why Sun A serves as the control.

How the model is set

Lag phase: curvature starts about 4.5 h after continuous unilateral blue light begins, as measured in cucumber hypocotyls (Photochemistry and Photobiology).

Light intensity: above about 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ the lag lengthens and the final curvature is reduced (Whippo & Hangarter, Plant Physiology 2003). Very dim light gives a weak response, so the model peaks near 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹.

Growth: the shaded flank elongates faster and the lit flank slower, as the Cholodny–Went hypothesis predicts (Plant Physiology).

Bend angle: the shoot's apex turns toward the light while gravitropism pulls it back toward vertical. Gravitropic correction follows a sine law (Galland et al., Planta 2002). The two combine into a photogravitropic set-point angle, weighted by the plant's photo- and gravi-sensitivities (Bastien, Douady & Moulia 2015). The model uses dθ/dt = Kp·g(I)·sin(φ − θ) − Kg·sin θ, with φ the light direction perceived one lag earlier.

The 360 h (15-day) timeline compresses seedling growth for display. The timing follows the figures above; the plant's size and the Kp/Kg ratio (3:1) are illustrative.
